HubSuite

A website for an AI ERP and CRM platform: design, live product demos and a WordPress site the client runs on their own

Platform WordPress, custom theme
Scope Design, animation, 20+ pages, WordPress theme, lead handling, documentation
Site hubsuite.net
App App Store, Google Play

The task

HubSuite is an ERP, CRM and AI platform for small and mid-sized businesses: 25+ modules, AI agents on the level of ChatGPT and Gemini, monitoring and checking of Prozorro public tenders

The company had a simple landing page and a WordPress version of the site by another developer that they did not like: heavy all-caps headings, flat blue and lime, dry, with no animation at all

An SEO agency had already approved the home page structure and a meta tag table for every page. They expected a site that looks strong and professional at first sight, with animation, light and dark themes and a proper mobile version

And the main thing: show the product itself. The system is big, and it is bought by business owners who need to understand what it does in a minute

Design

I started in Figma: six home page layouts, dark and light theme, Ukrainian and English, desktop and phone, with design variables and a clickable prototype

After the first review it was clear that static layouts did not carry what the client wanted, which was motion and a live product. So instead of polishing pictures we moved to a live site and kept refining it right in the browser, screen by screen

The brand colours stayed: dark blue and lime, with lime only as an accent. Dark is the main theme, light is a full theme with its own shades for every block

  • A stencil typeface in the hero, Geist for headings, Inter for text
  • Soft-cornered cards: light and border follow the cursor, nothing reacts to a finger on a phone
  • A background network that grows as you scroll and narrows into a single cord in the How it works block
  • A 3D logo intro on three.js: about three seconds and only on the first visit
  • A living logo in the header whose nodes react to the cursor
  • Smooth theme switching with View Transitions

Live demos

The key decision of the project: instead of screenshots or a video, the hero runs the product itself. It is HTML and JavaScript rebuilt from screenshots of the client's real system: the cursor opens modules, types in a chat, AI checks a package of tender documents, analytics numbers match between blocks

Every inner page has its own scene: tender monitoring, an approval sheet, a lead distribution report, control of ordered items. 31 scenes in total, with a cursor on desktop and finger taps on the phone

The points next to the program are synced with it: the one the demo is showing lights up, and a click on a point jumps the demo to that moment

Later the client brought their own 3D animations for the home, AI, tenders, industries and modules pages. I built them into the site: they adapt to the light theme, fit a screen of any height and stay sharp, and on a phone they show a zoomed-in part of the scene

Every demo uses job titles instead of people's names, and tender participants are made up. No real client data gets onto the site

Pages

Every page from the SEO agency's table, plus About and Pricing: a 17-section home page, Features, AI, Tenders, Industries, Pricing, About, Contacts, a blog with articles and an author page, four legal documents, documentation and a 404

So that inner pages would not repeat the home page, each one got its own centrepiece:

  • Tenders: a document package under review, risks get a status, a readiness ring fills up, the deadline counts down live
  • AI: a query console where AI reads sources in the system one by one and answers with steps
  • Modules: a builder for your own set of modules with a counter
  • Industries: a Sound familiar? self-check and a process drawn as a line with stations
  • Pricing: a 23-row comparison, onboarding and support based on the client's documents

The visitor's choice is not lost: the plan, module set or industry picked on a page arrives in the request together with the contacts

Requests

The Book a demo button opens a booking window like Calendly: a calendar with the managers' free time from Cal.com, details, review, confirmation. You can also skip the time, and a manager calls back

  • The meeting is booked in Cal.com right away, even when the event type requires its own fields
  • A request card in Telegram with history: changes edit the same message instead of piling up new ones
  • A shadow request: if someone filled in the form but did not confirm a time, the manager still sees the contact
  • An email to the person with the date, a join button and Add to calendar: Google, Apple, Outlook
  • People can view, change or cancel their request, and the change reaches Cal.com, Telegram and the admin
  • Where the visitor came from and from which page: UTM, ads, referring site
  • Bot protection with Cloudflare Turnstile

Documentation

Following the client's brief, the documentation became a blog of how-to guides: sections by module, each guide a separate article with its own address, a YouTube video on top, numbered steps with screenshots, search across all articles and private sections for customers only

Guides can be published by Claude. The site runs an MCP server, the theme gives it its own actions (sections, creating and updating guides, uploading screenshots) and a separate user that can touch nothing but the documentation. Whoever writes the text, the site turns it into the same step-by-step format

For search engines and AI: a title and schema for every guide, a sitemap, robots.txt that allows GPTBot, ClaudeBot and Perplexity, and llms.txt listing every guide

WordPress

The site is hand-coded, without a page builder, and my script builds the WordPress theme from it. Every text, button, image and section gets a key, so all of it can be edited

The client edits everything right on the page: texts, buttons and links, images and icons, SEO. Documents are edited like in Google Docs, with .docx import. Hide a page in the admin, and every button and menu item pointing to it disappears on its own

  • Messengers, social links and app links are changed in one place
  • SEO through Yoast without duplicate tags
  • Animations sleep off screen, three.js is trimmed to the classes in use, heavy demos load only when their section is near
  • Move to a new host: site copy, DNS, SSL, redirects from the old domain, email untouched

The app

HubSuite has a mobile app for iOS and Android with the same data as on the desktop: work chats and customer requests, notifications about new leads and tender deadlines, AI document checks from the phone, deals, tasks and sales analytics on the go

On the site it has its own section: two iPhones running the same live program as in the hero, with the proportions of a real phone, the island and a status bar. Next to them are the App Store and Google Play buttons, and the app is already in both stores

I also built the mobile version of the site itself separately instead of just shrinking it: its own first screen where the heading types out and slides up, the animation takes the full width and the buttons appear once it has played

The result

Instead of a landing page and a version they did not like, HubSuite has a site of twenty-plus pages where the product shows itself, and an admin where the client runs texts, requests and documentation without a developer

Requests from the site are booked into a manager's calendar and land in Telegram at once, and AI keeps the documentation growing

  • 20+ pages and 33 documentation guides
  • 31 live demo scenes, on desktop and on the phone
  • Dark and light theme
  • About five weeks from the first meeting to launch on the main domain
